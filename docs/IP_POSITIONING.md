# SleepBank — IP & Positioning Strategy

> **Purpose.** How SleepBank's assets are protected and positioned — the two
> separate moats (a branded wellness metric vs. a cleared clinical feature) and the
> guardrail that keeps them from contaminating each other. Companion to `FDA_PATH.md`.
>
> **Not legal advice.** Trademark availability and registrability must be confirmed
> via a USPTO TESS clearance search and a trademark attorney. Items marked
> **[VERIFY]** are my best guess.

## 1. Two moats, two tools — keep them separate

| Asset | What it is | Protected by | Regulatory status |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The Score** (Alert / Readiness / Energy metric) | Your proprietary, branded daily metric | **Trademark** (the name) + **trade secret** (the algorithm) | **General wellness** — must stay here |
| **Biofeedback breathing** (the pacer) | The paced-breathing relaxation intervention | **FDA 510(k) clearance** | **Regulated** — the clinical-credibility unlock |

This is the Whoop *Recovery* / Oura *Readiness* / Garmin *Body Battery* playbook: an
owned metric is the **consumer brand moat**; the cleared feature is the **clinical
moat** underneath. They reinforce each other — but you never entangle them (see §4).

## 2. Trademark — the name

- **You trademark the *name*, not the concept.** A score derived from health data
  is not ownable; the brand you build around it is.
- **Descriptive names are weak.** "Alert Score," "Readiness Score," "Recovery
  Score," "Sleep Score" are descriptive or generic — USPTO commonly refuses these or
  relegates them to the weak Supplemental Register, and "Sleep Score" is already
  industry-generic (Apple, Fitbit, Withings). **A coined or suggestive name is far
  more registrable and defensible** — Garmin's *Body Battery* is the model: invented,
  distinctive, ownable.
- **Avoid competitors' signature terms.** "Readiness" is Oura's territory and
  "Recovery" is Whoop's — a combined "Readiness Recovery Score" is the worst of both
  worlds (descriptiveness refusal *and* potential confusion/opposition from
  well-funded incumbents). **[VERIFY existing marks via TESS]**
- **First use in commerce builds rights.** The app already ships "Alert Score," which
  starts the clock on common-law rights — but a coined mark is what to *register*.
- **Action:** brainstorm coined candidates → TESS clearance search → attorney files
  the strongest one (intent-to-use or use-based).

## 3. Trade secret + copyright (the rest of the stack)

- **The scoring algorithm → trade secret, not patent.** A "score from health data"
  is an abstract idea and gets invalidated under *Alice*; secrecy protects it
  indefinitely and is cheaper. Keep the formula access-controlled and marked
  confidential; don't publish the exact weights.
- **The visual (the ring / UI) → copyright + trade dress.** The distinctive look of
  the energy ring is protectable as it becomes associated with the brand.

## 4. The guardrail — keep the Score in wellness

The single rule that protects both moats: **the Score must never make a clinical
claim.** The moment it says "you're recovered enough to [drive/operate]," screens
for a disorder, or otherwise diagnoses/treats, it becomes a regulated device output
(clinical decision support) and forfeits the wellness exemption.

- **Safe:** "your alertness is charged / draining," "you tend to dip mid-afternoon."
- **Not safe:** anything diagnostic, anything a clinician would act on medically.

**The biofeedback breathing is the regulated piece; the Score is not.** That clean
separation is exactly what lets the cleared feature lend credibility to the brand
without dragging the brand into device regulation.
